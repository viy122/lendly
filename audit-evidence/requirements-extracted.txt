I. INTRODUCTION
A. Purpose of the Document
This document is the preliminary Requirements Document for Lendly, a peer-to-peer item rental management system. It brings the business requirements, the development plan, and the test plan of the project together so that the development team, stakeholders, and testers share a common understanding of what the system must do and how it is expected to behave before development and testing proceed.
The document defines the functional and non-functional requirements, system users, and overall process flow of Lendly. It also presents the design direction, project timeline, and quality assurance approach that will guide the succeeding phases of the project. It serves as the primary reference for the development team, stakeholders, and testers throughout the development process.
B. Document Organization
The document is organized into nine parts, numbered with Roman numerals. Requirements are identified by unique IDs (FR-## for functional and NFR-## for non-functional requirements) so they can be cross-referenced between the requirements, design, and test sections. C. Topic 2: Item Rental Management System (Selected)
This is a peer-to-peer rental marketplace system that allows people to rent items from other individuals instead of purchasing items that they only need temporarily. The system connects item owners who want to earn from their unused items with renters who need those items for a specific period. Owners can post their items with details such as the item name, description, rental price, availability, and condition, while renters can browse, search, find nearby items on a map, and request to rent available items. The system also includes an admin who manages users, listings, and transactions to ensure that the platform operates properly.
Users
Owner/Renter — Uses the platform to either list items for rent or rent items from other users. As an Owner, the user can list and manage items, set rental prices and availability, manage rental requests, handle item pickup, hand-over, and return, and others. As a Renter, the user can search and browse available items, view item details, send rental requests, make payments, coordinate pickup or delivery, return rented items, etc.
Admin — Manages user accounts, item listings, rental transactions, and reports or disputes within the platform.
Key Features
[TABLE]
Feature | Description
User Registration and Login | Allows owners and renters to create accounts and securely access the platform, entering the interface of the role they select at login.
Owner Item Listing and Management | Enables owners to post, edit, or remove item listings available for rent.
Item Search and Browsing | Allows renters to search and filter available items based on their needs.
Map / Find Items Near You | Allows renters to see available items on an interactive map, use their current location to filter by distance and sort by nearest, and search by keyword and category.

Payment and Transaction Management | Handles payment processing and records completed transactions.
Item Pickup or Delivery | Allows both parties to coordinate how the item will be handed over.
Rental Tracking | Monitors the status of an ongoing rental, including upcoming due dates.

Process Flow
The owner and renter register and log in to the platform, selecting at login whether to enter as an Owner or as a Renter.
The owner lists an item for rent, providing complete details, pricing, and availability.
The renter searches for an item, including by finding items near their location on a map, and sends a rental request for the desired period.
The owner reviews the request and approves or declines it.
Once approved, the system confirms the booking, prompts for agreement terms, and processes the payment.
The renter may cancel the request or booking prior to pickup or delivery, subject to the platform’s cancellation policy and applicable fees.
The owner and renter coordinate the pickup or delivery of the item.
The system tracks the rental period until the item is due for return.
The owner confirms the item’s return and condition, closing the transaction.
Both parties may rate and review each other, and the completed rental is recorded in their history.
The admin monitors platform activity and resolves any reported issues or disputes.

E. Selected Topic and Rationale
Among the three proposed system topics, the group has decided to pursue the Item Rental Management System as the final project topic. This topic was chosen because it offers a well-rounded scope that allows the team to apply and demonstrate a wide range of system development and quality assurance concepts, from user account management and transaction processing to booking, payment, and dispute resolution.
It also presents a relevant and practical solution to a common need: allowing individuals to rent out unused items and helping others access items they only need temporarily, without the cost of buying them outright. With its clearly defined users, features, and process flow, the group believes this topic will provide a solid and manageable foundation for the succeeding phases of the project.
III. BUSINESS REQUIREMENTS: SYSTEM OVERVIEW
A. Project Overview
Lendly is a peer-to-peer rental marketplace system that allows people to rent items from other individuals instead of purchasing items they only need temporarily. The system connects item owners who want to earn from their unused items with renters who need those items for a specific period. Owners can post their items with details such as the item name, description, rental price, availability, and condition, while renters can browse, search, find nearby items on a map, and request to rent available items. An admin manages users, listings, and transactions to ensure that the platform operates properly.
The purpose of the business requirements in Parts III to VI is to clearly define the business and system requirements of the Item Rental Management System so that all stakeholders share a common understanding of the platform’s intended functionality, quality attributes, and end-to-end processes before development and testing proceed.
B. Scope
In Scope
User registration, authentication, role selection at login, and account management for owners and renters.
Owner item listing creation, editing, and management, including photos, pricing, availability, and location.
Item search, browsing, and detailed item viewing for renters.
Map-based item discovery for renters, including viewing listings on an interactive map, using the renter’s current location, distance filtering, nearest-first sorting, and keyword and category search.
Rental request submission, approval, and booking confirmation, including acceptance of rental terms.
Rental cancellation by the renter, including enforcement of the applicable cancellation policy and computation of cancellation fees as specified in the rental terms and agreement.
Payment and transaction recording.
Coordination of item pickup or delivery between owner and renter.
In-app messaging that allows owners and renters to communicate with each other regarding rental requests, coordination, and inquiries.
Rental tracking, due-date reminders, and return/condition confirmation.
Ratings and reviews between owners and renters.
Rental history and owner earnings/transaction records.
Admin management of users, listings, transactions, reports, and disputes.
Out of Scope
Integration with a live third-party online payment gateway, unless confirmed as part of finalized requirements.
Hardware requirements and underlying network infrastructure.
Integration with third-party delivery or courier services.

D. Assumptions and Constraints
Assumptions
Users have access to a device and a stable internet connection to use the platform.
Users provide accurate and truthful information during registration and item listing.
Registered email addresses are valid and accessible for verification and notification purposes.
Renters who want to use location-based features allow their browser to share their location, and owners provide an accurate location for each item listing.
Constraints
Online payment gateway integration is dependent on confirmation as part of the finalized project requirements.
Item condition checking upon return relies on manual inspection by the owner; the system does not perform automated damage detection.
Map and location-based features depend on the user’s browser supporting geolocation and on the availability of a map service; when the user’s location is not shared, distance-based filtering and sorting are not available.
Coordination of delivery is limited to information exchange within the platform; the system does not manage third-party courier logistics.
Rental cancellations are only permitted prior to item hand-over; cancellation fees, when applicable, are computed automatically based on the rules defined in the rental terms and agreement.
IV. REQUIREMENTS DEFINITION AND QUALITY FRAMEWORK
A. Definition of a Requirement
A requirement is a documented statement of a condition or capability that a system must have in order to solve a problem or achieve an objective. The IEEE Standard Glossary of Software Engineering Terminology (IEEE Std 610.12) defines a requirement as:
a condition or capability needed by a user to solve a problem or achieve an objective;
a condition or capability that must be met or possessed by a system or system component to satisfy a contract, standard, specification, or other formally imposed document; and
a documented representation of a condition or capability as in (1) or (2).
Taken together, a requirement in this document has three aspects: it expresses a need of a stakeholder, it states an obligation that the system must satisfy, and it is recorded in a documented form that can be traced and verified. A requirement document collects these statements so that stakeholders, developers, and testers work from the same agreed baseline.
To be useful for development and for software quality assurance, each requirement in this document is written to be:
Clear and unambiguous — it can be read in only one way by all stakeholders.
Verifiable — it can be checked by inspection, demonstration, or test, so that a test case can be written against it.
Consistent — it does not conflict with other requirements.
Complete — it states everything needed to implement and verify it.
Traceable — it carries a unique ID that links it to the process flows, design, and test scope.
B. Requirement Classification and Conventions
Requirements in this document are divided into the following categories:

C. Software Quality Factors
Functional requirements describe what a system does, but they do not describe how well it must do it. For this reason, requirements also need to be defined for each quality factor that applies to the product. Quality factors give the team measurable qualities to design for and verify, and they connect the requirements directly to the testing activities of the Test Plan.
This document uses the software quality factors of McCall’s quality model, which groups eleven factors into three categories according to the stage of the product’s life they concern: product operation (using the product), product revision (changing the product), and product transition (moving the product to a new environment).
[TABLE]
Quality Factor | Definition
Product Operation Factors: How well the system performs its intended function when it is operated.
Correctness | The extent to which the system satisfies its specification and fulfills the users’ objectives.
Reliability | The extent to which the system can be expected to perform its intended function with the required precision.
Efficiency | The amount of computing resources and code required by the system to perform its function.
Integrity | The extent to which access to the software or data by unauthorized persons can be controlled.
Usability | The effort required to learn, operate, prepare input for, and interpret the output of the system.
Product Revision Factors: How easily the system can be changed once it is in operation.
Maintainability | The effort required to locate and fix an error in the system.
Flexibility | The effort required to modify an operational system.
Testability | The effort required to test the system to ensure that it performs its intended function.
Product Transition Factors: How well the system adapts to a new environment.
Portability | The effort required to transfer the system from one hardware or software environment to another.
Reusability | The extent to which the system, or parts of it, can be reused in other applications.
Interoperability | The effort required to couple the system with another system.
D. Emphasis and Applicability of Quality Factors in Lendly
Requirement documents are expected to differ in the emphasis they place on the various quality factors, depending on the purpose of the system and the needs of its users. This difference in emphasis is also what allows the requirement documents of different systems to be compared with one another. Not all of the factors are necessarily represented in a given document: a factor may receive strong emphasis, only a narrow treatment, or no dedicated requirement at all.
The table below states how each quality factor applies to Lendly and points to the non-functional requirements that address it (Part VI). Because Lendly is a marketplace that handles user accounts, bookings, and payment records between strangers, the strongest emphasis is placed on correctness, reliability, integrity, and usability.
[TABLE]
Quality Factor | Applicable | Emphasis | Rationale for Lendly | Related NFRs

A. System Design
A.1 Architecture Overview
Lendly is a web-based system accessed by Owners, Renters, and the Admin through standard web browsers on desktop and mobile devices. B. Process Flow
This section describes the end-to-end process flow of the system, from account creation through the completion of a rental transaction. Figure 2 gives an overview of the complete flow, followed by the step-by-step description of each flow.
[IMAGE media/image17.png]
Figure 2. End-to-end process flow of a Lendly rental, with alternative and supporting flows
B.1 User Registration and Login Flow
This flow establishes how individuals create and access their accounts as either an Owner, a Renter, or both.
Step-by-Step Process:
The user accesses the platform and selects the “Sign Up” option.
The user enters the required registration details: full name, email address, and password.
The system validates the entered information and checks for existing accounts using the same email address.
The system sends a verification link or code to the user’s registered email address.
The user confirms the verification link or code within the given validity period.
Upon successful verification, the account is activated and the user is redirected to the login page.
Returning users log in using their registered email and password; a “Forgot Password” option is available for password recovery. After entering their credentials, users select whether to log in as an Owner or as a Renter, and the system opens only the interface and modules of the selected role.
Process Outcome: Only verified individuals gain access to the platform, establishing a reliable foundation for all subsequent listing and rental activities.
B.2 Item Listing and Management Flow (Owner)
This flow describes how an owner posts an item and keeps its listing up to date.
Step-by-Step Process:
The owner logs in and navigates to the “My Listings” section of the dashboard.
The owner selects “Add New Item” to begin creating a listing.
The owner enters the item’s name, category, description, condition, rental price, available dates, etc.
The owner sets the item’s location so that the listing can appear on the map.
The owner uploads one or more photos of the item.
The owner submits the listing for publishing.
The system validates that all required fields are complete and publishes the listing to the marketplace.
The owner may edit, pause, or remove the listing at any time from the dashboard.
Process Outcome: Renters are able to browse accurate, up-to-date, and complete item listings maintained directly by the item owners.
B.3 Item Search and Browsing Flow (Renter)
This flow describes how a renter finds an item that fits their needs, either through the search list or through the map.
Step-by-Step Process:
The renter accesses the search or browse page of the platform, or opens the map view.
The renter enters keywords and/or applies filters such as category, price range, and location.
The system displays matching item listings along with key details (photo, name, price, availability, etc).
In the map view, available items are shown as pins on an interactive map.
The renter may choose “Use my location” so that, with the browser’s permission, the map centers on and marks the renter’s current position, and may limit the results to a chosen distance radius.
Once the location is set, the system sorts the listings from nearest to farthest; keyword and category filters continue to apply.
Each pin shows a preview of the item; selecting it opens the item’s detail page.
The renter selects an item to view its full details, including description, photos, availability calendar, and others.
Process Outcome: Renters can quickly locate suitable items, including those nearest to them, without unnecessary friction, improving the overall marketplace experience.
B.4 Rental Request, Approval, and Booking Flow
This flow governs how a rental transaction is initiated and confirmed between an owner and a renter.
Step-by-Step Process:
The renter selects the desired rental period and submits a rental request for the item.
The system notifies the owner of the new incoming request.
The owner reviews the request details and either approves or declines it.
If approved, both the owner and renter are prompted to review and accept the rental terms and agreement.
Once both parties accept the terms, the system generates a confirmed booking with the computed rental cost.
Both parties receive a booking confirmation notification.
Process Outcome: Rental transactions are formalized only after mutual agreement, protecting the interests of both the item owner and the renter.
B.5 Payment and Transaction Flow
This flow describes how rental fees are calculated, collected, and recorded.
Step-by-Step Process:
The system computes the total rental fee based on the agreed rental duration and the item’s rate.
The renter completes payment through the platform’s supported payment method.
The system records the transaction and generates a receipt for both the owner and the renter.
The system updates the item’s status to “Reserved” for the duration of the confirmed booking.
Process Outcome: All payments are accurately recorded and traceable, giving both parties a transparent transaction history.
B.6 Item Pickup or Delivery Flow
This flow describes how a rented item is physically transferred from the owner to the renter.
Step-by-Step Process:
The system notifies both parties of the confirmed pickup or delivery schedule.
The owner and renter coordinate the exchange of the item, whether in person or through an agreed delivery arrangement.
The owner marks the item as “Handed Over” once it has been released to the renter while the renter marks the item as “Received” once the item is successfully received.
The system updates the rental status to “Ongoing.”
Process Outcome: The hand-over of the item is clearly logged, giving both parties a documented starting point for the rental period.
B.7 Rental Tracking and Return Flow
This flow covers how an active rental is monitored and how the item is returned and closed out.
Step-by-Step Process:
The system tracks the rental period and displays its real-time status (Ongoing, Due Soon, or Overdue).
The system sends reminder notifications as the due date approaches.
The renter returns the item to the owner on or before the agreed due date.
The owner inspects the returned item and records its condition in the system.
The system closes the rental transaction and archives it in both users’ rental history.
Process Outcome: Rentals are consistently monitored from start to finish, reducing disputes over due dates and item condition.
B.8 Ratings and Reviews Flow
This flow allows both parties to provide feedback after a completed transaction.
Step-by-Step Process:
After a rental transaction is closed, the system prompts both the owner and renter to optionally rate and review each other.
Each user submits a star rating and an optional written comment.
The system publishes the submitted rating and review to the respective user’s public profile.
Process Outcome: A transparent reputation system is maintained, helping future users make informed rental decisions.
B.9 Admin Management and Dispute Resolution Flow
This flow describes how the admin oversees platform activity and resolves reported issues.
Step-by-Step Process:
The admin monitors the dashboard for user accounts, item listings, and ongoing transactions.
The admin reviews any items, users, or transactions flagged or reported by owners and renters.
The admin investigates the dispute by examining transaction details, or any submitted evidence.
The admin takes appropriate action, such as mediation, a refund recommendation, or account suspension.
The admin closes the report and notifies the involved parties of the resolution.
Process Outcome: Reported issues are addressed consistently and fairly, maintaining trust and accountability across the marketplace.
B.10 Rental Cancellation Flow
This flow describes how a renter cancels a rental request or confirmed booking, and how any applicable cancellation fee is applied in accordance with the rental terms and agreement.
Step-by-Step Process:
The renter initiates cancellation of a rental request or a confirmed booking, prior to item hand-over, from the booking or rental details page.
The system checks the timing of the cancellation against the cancellation policy stated in the rental terms and agreement.
If the cancellation falls within a chargeable period, the system computes and applies the corresponding cancellation fee.
The system notifies the owner that the rental has been cancelled and reverts the item’s status to “Available.”
The system records the cancellation, including any cancellation fee applied and/or reason, in the transaction and rental history of both parties.
Process Outcome: Cancellations are handled transparently and consistently, with cancellation fees applied only in accordance with the agreed rental terms, protecting the interests of both the owner and the renter.
C. UI Design
C.1 Design Considerations
The initial user interface of Lendly is designed around the quality attributes and functional needs defined in Parts V and VI. The following considerations guide the UI/UX design task (WBS 1.5):
Simple and intuitive flows — owners can list an item, and renters can complete a booking, with minimal steps (NFR-09).
Visible status — request status, item availability, and rental status are shown clearly wherever they apply (FR-08, FR-13, FR-19).
Map-based discovery — renters can see available items as pins on a map, optionally use their current location, and preview an item and its distance before opening it; browsing and searching remain available when location is not shared (FR-48 to FR-53, NFR-20).
Clear, actionable error messages — errors tell the user what went wrong and how to fix it (NFR-10).
Terms before booking — the rental terms and agreement, including the cancellation policy and fees, are shown before both parties accept and the booking is finalized (FR-15, FR-34, FR-35).
Transparent costs — the computed rental cost and any cancellation fee are displayed to the user (FR-16, FR-35).
Role-appropriate navigation — Owners, Renters, and the Admin see only the areas and actions available to their role (NFR-07). A user who is both an Owner and a Renter selects a role at login and enters a separate interface for that role, with no merged Owner/Renter view (FR-03).
Responsive layout — screens adapt to different devices and work across major web browsers like Chrome (NFR-15).

B. Test Methodology
B.1 Overview
The team will use manual test cases, as well as an automated backend testing tool like Postman, and an automated frontend testing tool such as Selenium to validate the core features of the Item Rental Management System. Testing will be conducted in cycles aligned with the development milestones, with each cycle focusing on newly completed features to ensure their functionality, reliability, and proper integration.
B.2 Test Levels
[TABLE]
Level | Description
Unit Testing | Validation of individual functions such as registration form validation, item listing form validation, booking status logic, etc.
Integration Testing | Verification that the listing, booking, terms and agreement, and notification components interact correctly with each other, including the use of email service for verification and password recovery as well as map service for displaying location information.
System Testing | End-to-end user journey testing from browsing an item through booking, terms acceptance, and return confirmation.
B.3 Bug Triage
The team conducts a bug triage meeting at the end of each testing cycle. Findings are reviewed collectively and a fix plan is assigned to the developer. If the cycle passes acceptance criteria, the team proceeds to the next. All defects must be tracked in the traceability matrix.
B.4 Acceptance Criteria
[TABLE]
Entry Criteria | Exit Criteria
Test data prepared and accessible. Entry criteria for the start of testing is a hand-off from the developers through email. | No open Severity 1 or Severity 2 defects. 100% test case execution completed. Minimum 95% test pass rate achieved.
Suspension Criteria and Resumption Requirements. Testing is suspended if 40% or more of test cases fail within a cycle. Testing resumes only after the developer resolves all failing cases and issues a new hand-off.
B.5 Test Completion and Sign-off
Mandatory run rate: 90% (exceptions require documented justification)
Mandatory pass rate: 95%
Final sign-off by Project Leader: Dorina Cables
